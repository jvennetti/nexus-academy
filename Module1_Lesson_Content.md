## Lesson 2 — The One-Person Startup Era Has Officially Begun
**Rho | ~7 min**

▶ [Watch Now](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rsSUIvAkjvk)

Sam Altman made a bet in a group chat with tech CEOs: the first one-person billion-dollar company is coming, and it's only possible because of AI. In 2024, that sounded absurd. Today, it's already happening — a 50-person company hit $100M in revenue and a $2.1B valuation. A solo founder crossed $1M in year one with no team.

The constraint that made small-team companies impossible at real scale is shrinking fast.

**Key takeaways:**
- A traditional software company at that revenue typically runs 200–400 people. Gamma: $100M revenue, $2.1B valuation, ~50 people. Revenue per employee = $2M.
- The logic isn't about being solo forever — it's about hiring what AI can't do
- Claude Code specifically changed who can build: non-technical founders can now direct product without waiting on developers
- AI still can't replace strategic judgment, high-trust relationships, and knowing when to pivot

## Lesson 3 — How to Win With AI in 2026
**Alex Hormozi | ~15 min**

▶ [Watch Now](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9q5ojtkqsBs)

Hormozi's take is blunter than most: AI will never be worse than it is right now, and if you're not treating this as your top priority, you're already behind. This video reframes AI from a productivity tool into a competitive position, and makes the case for why being early matters so much more than being perfect.

The key conceptual shift he introduces: **stop thinking about prompting, start thinking about context systems.** The people winning with AI aren't the ones writing clever prompts, they're the ones who've built the context infrastructure that makes every prompt more powerful.

**Key takeaways:**
- According to Hormozi, the most flexible system survives... this is business Darwinism in real time
- Starting a new company AI-first is easier than converting an existing one (there is no legacy structure to fight)
- The skill stack compounds: every AI capability you add gives disproportionate leverage over competitors
- Revenue per employee is the new metric to watch. Hormozi's companies are running in the millions per head

## Module 1 Challenge

**The Real-World Scenario:**

You've just been introduced to a potential client — a local law firm with 8 attorneys. They've heard about AI but have done nothing with it. Before your second conversation, they send you a quick message: *"We're open to exploring AI, but honestly we don't know where to start or if it's even relevant for us. Can you send something over?"*

**Your challenge:**

Using only what you learned in Module 1 (no tools yet — just your thinking), draft a 3–5 sentence reply that:

1. Gives them one concrete, credible example of how a firm their size has already used AI to save meaningful time or reduce cost
2. Positions this as the start of something, not a sales pitch
3. Sounds like a human wrote it, not a consultant

Write it out before you move to Module 2. Keep it. You'll come back to it.

> **Integration note:** Legal & Professional Services is one of the highest-leverage AI adoption areas — case research, contract review, billing capture. You don't need to know the technical details yet. You just need to know there are incredible needs to be met.
